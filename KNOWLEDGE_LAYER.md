# Evidence knowledge layer

Registry: backend/src/evidence.ts. Retrieved 29 October 2026 from publisher pages, PubMed and the published article record. Original short summaries only; no scraped full-text archive. Four published studies, with DOI, URL, study design, population and limitations. The registry is bundled/versioned with the Worker; no unreviewed web results enter inference at runtime.

- PREDIMED 2018 reanalysis, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1800389: dietary-pattern research; important allocation/reanalysis caveat. The abstract/full text was not fully retrievable in this environment, so the summary is deliberately limited to the study's topic and population, without quantitative efficacy claims.
- DASH 1997, DOI 10.1056/NEJM199704173361601: randomized feeding trial; eight-week blood-pressure outcome. Not automatic dairy recommendations or treatment targets.
- Borghi 2002, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa010369: recurrent calcium-oxalate stones with idiopathic hypercalciuria in men. Publisher study-population/methods evidence retrieved; no generalized kidney-stone diet or quantitative prescription derived.
- Paluch 2022, DOI 10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9: prospective cohort meta-analysis; association and age dependence, not causation or a personal exercise target. Publisher PDF/search record retrieved; PMC full-page access was challenged, so no full-text ingestion claimed.

Explicit research/source questions retrieve a short registry summary and source link. The general dietary-pattern entry is supplied to plan selection as contextual evidence; catalog suitability and user exclusions always take precedence. No recipe is described as clinically validated by these studies.

Health-specific entries are educational and cannot remove a planning hold. Activity-dose generation is withheld because the existing profile lacks age, baseline activity, mobility/injury details and contraindication review. The app does not assume adult eligibility or invent missing health data. A complete personalized activity plan needs that profile extension and clinician-reviewed applicability rules; downloading studies is not equivalent to validating them for an individual.
